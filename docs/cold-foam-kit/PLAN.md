# Tarte "Iced Black at Home" Kit — Commercial Plan

**Product:** a retail kit for making a Tarte iced long black at home — Tarte
house-blend grind (roasted by Parallel Roasters) + Tarte cold foam topper in
the three venue flavours (vanilla bean, dulce de leche, matcha) + brew/froth
instructions, with a branded electric mini-frother in the gift tier.

**Status:** plan drafted 14 Aug 2026. Supporting research:
[research-stability.md](research-stability.md) (foam science, stabilizers,
shelf life, QC) and [research-commercial.md](research-commercial.md) (formats,
regulation, co-packers, pricing precedents). Financial context from Xero P&L
FY Aug 2025–Jul 2026.

---

## 1. Why this, why now

- **Category white space in Australia.** Cold foam creamers are the
  fastest-growing US coffee category (International Delight, Coffee mate,
  Dunkin' all launched 2024–25; cold foam is Starbucks' fastest-growing
  customisation at +23% YoY) and **nothing is ranged in Coles/Woolworths**.
  TikTok search interest for cold foam is +104.7% YoY and Australians are
  actively searching "cold foam australia woolworths coles".
- **The at-home shift is a tailwind, not a threat.** 55% of Australian
  consumers say café prices have pushed them toward at-home coffee; iced long
  blacks retail at A$6.50–8.00. A kit that delivers the Tarte drink at
  ~A$4–5/serve captures spend that is leaving cafés anyway — and Tarte's own
  queue is the acquisition channel.
- **Tarte already has the product in-venue.** All three cold foam flavours are
  live menu add-ons with trained baristas and proven demand. This is
  productising an existing recipe, not inventing one.
- **Existing retail infrastructure.** The Currumbin Market did A$346k in the
  last FY — a ready high-traffic retail channel — while general retail sales
  are only ~A$29k/yr, so the kit builds a genuinely new revenue line without
  cannibalising anything.
- **Local precedent.** Mr. Consistent (Gold Coast) scaled a hospitality-born
  bottled beverage component to national DTC; Gelato Messina sold
  dulce-de-leche drink kits at A$85–159; ST. ALi put both beans and a chilled
  dairy coffee product into supermarkets. The hospitality→FMCG ladder is
  proven, including from this postcode.

**Business context (Xero, FY Aug 2025–Jul 2026):** revenue A$5.61M, gross
profit A$3.70M, net profit A$267k. Coffee + drink sales A$1.53M; Parallel
Roasters bean spend ~A$197k/yr (purchasing leverage for a retail grind SKU).
The phased plan below keeps Phase 1 cash exposure under ~A$15k, inside normal
working-capital tolerance, before any larger commitment.

**Cross-venue mechanic:** flavours map to venues — vanilla bean = Bakery

### 2.2 Coffee component (near-zero burden)

Parallel Roasters supplies **finished, retail-ready labelled bags** ("Roasted
by Parallel for Tarte"). Ground coffee is licence-exempt low-risk food in QLD;
Best Before + Country of Origin labelling only; ambient; no allergens. Grind
spec'd coarse for immersion/cold brew with a French-press-and-fridge method on
the recipe card, so no customer equipment beyond a jar is assumed.

### 2.3 Foam component — formulation (the heart of the product)

Base architecture (from the stability research, §"Formulation Synthesis"):

- **Sweet-cream hybrid**: cream + skim milk to **8–12% fat, ≥3% protein**
  (optionally +0.5–1% micellar casein/MPC). Deliberately outside the 3–15%
  pure-milk "dead zone" logic: enough casein for cold protein films, enough
  cold solid fat for partial-coalescence body — the Starbucks sweet-cream
  architecture, and the fat also shields proteins against coffee acid.
- **Sugars ≤10–12%** — sugar slows drainage (stability ↑) but kills
  foamability past ~15%.
- **High-acyl gellan 0.02–0.04% fluid gel** (or xanthan 0.05–0.1% in the
  in-café phase): yield stress suspends bubbles, stops drainage, and keeps a
  sharp foam/coffee layer without perceptible thickness.
- **Acid buffer — non-optional**: dipotassium phosphate or trisodium citrate
  0.1–0.3%. Black coffee at pH ~4.8–5.2 sits inside casein's aggregation
  onset (~pH 5.3); the buffer is why commercial cold foam cans don't feather.
  An iced long black is the *worst* case (higher titratable acidity than cold
  brew at the same pH) — this product must be engineered for it.
- **Acid-facing film reinforcement**: pectin or PGA (0.05–0.1%) — *not*
  carrageenan, which loses function at falling pH (Starbucks' RTD nitro sweet
  cream uses pectin for exactly this reason).
- All additives are FSANZ-permitted classes (Standard 1.3.1); verify Schedule
  15 limits for the dairy-drink category during formulation lock.

**Flavour-variant engineering:**

| Variant | Risk | Handling |
|---|---|---|
| Vanilla bean | Low | Water/PG-based or spray-dried vanilla (never oil-based — oils are foam breakers); dose for end-of-code flavour (vanillin fades); bean specks are cosmetic-only |
| Dulce de leche | Moderate | Cap flavour paste at 8–10% of base and total sugars ≤10–12%; expect ~10–20% lower overrun (compensate: longer froth or +0.01% gellan); density-check that foam still floats on ~1.02–1.04 g/mL coffee; buffer especially important (Maillard acids + extra calcium) |
| Matcha | Highest | Pre-disperse under high shear, never dry-dose (clumps are bridging antifoams — ~70% failure rate unsifted); gellan fluid gel mandatory for suspension; opaque pack (chlorophyll browning); plan ~2/3 the shelf life of vanilla pending trials |

### 2.4 Aeration method

**Electric handheld mini-frother** (private-label, landed cost <A$8, retails
A$15–20 in the gift kit): 20–30 s froth to ~50–150% overrun, matches the
in-café texture, and is the method TikTok already taught the market.
**Explicitly not N₂O chargers**: nitrous oxide is Schedule 6 / restricted in
QLD with 18+ retail rules — a compliance and brand-safety non-starter for a
consumer kit. Recipe card teaches pour order (foam last, over the back of a
spoon) — this measurably extends the layer.

## 3. Stability & quality program (how quality holds)

The kit only works if the foam a customer makes in week 2 of the bottle
matches the café. Three lines of defence:

### 3.1 Formulate for end-of-code, not day 1
- The governing spec is **overrun and drainage half-life at end of shelf
  life**, not at production.
- **Lipolysis is the #1 foam killer**: free fatty acids from psychrotroph
  lipases destroy protein films. Intake spec on every cream/milk lot: ADV/FFA
  + psychrotroph count (this also becomes a supplier requirement on Norco/
  Maleny/whoever supplies the dairy).
- End-of-life pH spec ≥6.5 — a slightly soured base plus acidic coffee tips
  casein over the aggregation threshold even when the product still smells fine.
- "Shake well before use" is designed in (fluid gel re-disperses cream line),
  not fought.

### 3.2 Batch QC bench (runs in a café kitchen, ~30 min/batch)
1. **Overrun** by cup-weight (scale + fixed 100 mL cup) — target 65–150%,
   variant within −20% of unflavoured control.
2. **Drainage half-life** at 5 °C (funnel over graduated cylinder) — spec
   ≥10 min.
3. **Foam height retention** on a standard iced test drink at 0/5/10/15 min
   (photo log).
4. **Foam-on-acid challenge** — foam dosed onto pH-4.8 citrate water (or a
   reference Tarte cold brew batch) at 5 °C: no visible feathering at 15 min;
   layer half-life on coffee ≥70% of half-life on water. This test exists
   because the product tops an iced black.
5. Base viscosity (flow cup), pH, °Brix each batch.
6. Variant-specific: matcha sediment height at 7/14/21 d and colour drift;
   dulce float check; vanilla end-of-code flavour.
7. Weekly foam re-test of retained samples through end of code; end-of-code
   organoleptic sign-off before any shelf-life claim is printed.

### 3.3 Shelf life by phase
- **Phase 1 (in-café fresh)**: cold-blended from pasteurised inputs, 7-day
  use-by, fridge grab-and-go. No re-pasteurisation = no new process risk.
- **Phase 2 (co-packed ESL)**: extended-shelf-life processing (microfiltration
  or high-heat ESL) → **21–45 day refrigerated code** — the sweet spot for a
  branded chilled creamer; UHT actually *improves* cold foamability (denatured
  whey) but adds cooked notes and a plasmin age-gelation ceiling, so ESL is
  preferred while the brand story is "fresh local dairy".
- **Gift/ambient tier (parallel track)**: powdered foam sachets (gasified
  spray-dried creamer tech, 12–24 month ambient life) — hardest technically
  for *cold* reconstitution (needs cold-soluble proteins + methylcellulose),
  so treat as an R&D option for the national gifting play, not a launch
  blocker. Matcha suits powder best.

## 4. Regulatory pathway (Queensland)

The kit structure is regulatorily convenient: the grind is licence-exempt, so
all compliance collapses onto the foam.

| Phase | Channel | What's required |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Made & packed in-café, sold same premises (incl. Currumbin Market stall — confirm "same business" treatment with council) | City of Gold Coast licence check/amendment at Burleigh (~20 days lead); Standard 3.2.2A Cat 1 (FSS cert current); partial labelling exemption applies but allergen info mandatory — label fully anyway (see below) |
| 2 | Co-packed bottles, sold at venues + local DTC | **Co-packing sidesteps SFPQ dairy accreditation entirely** — Norco is the accredited processor; Tarte is brand owner. Full FSANZ labelling; recall plan; product liability cover at A$10–20M (wholesale buyers expect it) |
| 3 | Wholesale / broader DTC | Recall system live; cold chain ≤5 °C evidenced (data loggers); chilled DTC realistically capped to SEQ/northern-NSW next-day zones until an ambient SKU exists |

**Decisions to get in writing (both are cheap phone calls first):**
1. **SFPQ determination** (07 3253 9800) on the exact Phase-1 process —
   cold-blending bought-in pasteurised milk/cream with sugar/flavour, no
   re-pasteurisation, retail from own premises. Analogy to the milkshake/
   gelato retail exemption says no accreditation needed; NSW treats flavoured
   milk as licensable processing, so do not assume. Ask per-channel (retail /
   market stall / DTC).
2. **City of Gold Coast** on (a) the Burleigh licence covering
   manufacturing for the other two venues + market stall, and (b) whether
   repacking Parallel's bulk grind needs the manufacturing extension (moot if
   Parallel supplies retail-ready bags — preferred).

**Labelling (do fully from day 1 even where exemptions apply):** NIP per serve
+ per 100 mL; bold "Contains: Milk" (PEAL); **Use By** on chilled foam, Best
Before on grind/powder; lot ID; name + address; characterising ingredient %;
storage directions; CoOL. Each inner component of the kit labelled + outer box
not obscuring mandatory info (QLD Health "Label Buster" as the checklist).
Matcha foam is standard food (no formulated-caffeinated-beverage issues).

## 5. Manufacturing & supply chain

- **Phase 1 — in-café production** at Burleigh (largest kitchen, existing
  prep discipline): cold-blend batches, bottle, date-stamp, distribute to all
  three venues + market with existing runs. Equipment: essentially nil beyond
  bottles, labels, a stick blender and the QC bench (~A$1–2k incl. pH meter,
  refractometer, scale).
- **Phase 2 — Norco Foods contract manufacturing, Labrador (Gold Coast)**:
  fresh-milk plant minutes from all venues, explicitly courts small brands,
  formats 150 mL–1,000 L, already SFPQ-accredited. Get pilot-run size and
  tolling $/L quoted for the buffered sweet-cream base in PET. Fallbacks:
  OzScientific (dairy NPD formulation support), Omniblend (VIC, small-run UHT
  if an ambient bottle is ever wanted).
- **Cold chain (Phase 2+ DTC):** GC-local HACCP refrigerated couriers (Cool
  Couriers, Chill, All Purpose) + insulated liner/gel-pack packs with data
  loggers for DTC; Australian Cold Chain Guidelines 2017 as the SOP baseline.
- **Frother:** private-label handheld units, landed <A$8; order with long lead
  for the gift-kit Christmas window.
- **Do not build a pasteurisation line** (A$50–150k installed) unless Phase 2
  volumes prove out and Norco tolling economics disappoint.

**Phase 1 — In-café pilot (Oct 2026, ahead of summer)**
- Fresh 7-day-code foam bottles in grab-and-go fridges beside an ambient
  gift-shelf display; kits at all three venues + Currumbin Market stall.

  reel; the daily queue is the acquisition channel.
- Track in this system: kits/refills by venue and flavour, wastage on
  short-code stock, refill repeat rate.
- Gate to Phase 2: ≥25 kits/wk sustained + refill rate ≥30% + wastage <15%.

**Phase 2 — Co-pack + DTC (early 2027)**
- Norco ESL run (21–45 day code) once velocity is proven; full labelling;
  recall plan; SEQ chilled DTC via refrigerated courier.
- Christmas 2026 learnings → gift kit v2; frother volume order.

**Phase 3 — Scale (mid/late 2027, only if Phase 2 proves)**
- Refill-bottle wholesale to select SEQ grocers/delis (ST. ALi template);
  powdered ambient sachet R&D for national gifting; seasonal LTO flavours
  (the US category's playbook) using the same base.

## 8. Risks & mitigations

| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| Foam feathers/collapses on customers' (variable) home coffee | Buffered formulation + foam-on-acid QC gate + pour-order instructions; recipe card standardises the brew (ratio, steep time) to bound the acidity range |
| Matcha SKU quality drift (sediment, colour) | Fluid-gel suspension, opaque bottle, shorter printed code, sediment QC; if it still underperforms, matcha becomes the powder-format SKU first |
| Short 7-day code drives Phase-1 wastage | Small daily batches from existing prep routine; make-to-par using this system's wastage tracking; foam also usable in-venue (zero-waste valve — it's the same product baristas serve) |
| SFPQ rules Phase 1 needs dairy accreditation | Determination requested before launch; worst case, jump straight to Norco co-pack (Phase 2 structure) and keep Phase 1 to in-cup foam + grind-only kits |
| Cold chain abuse in QLD summer (DTC) | Phase-gate DTC behind co-packed ESL product; gel-pack + liner validation with data loggers; SEQ-only radius for chilled |
| Category gets crowded (Danone/Nestlé land in AU) | Move now (12–24 month window); position on fresh local dairy + café authenticity, which imports can't copy; three-venue flavour story is defensible |
| Demand disappoints | Phase-1 exposure capped ~A$15k; product doubles as existing in-café stock; grind bags and frothers sell standalone |
