# Research appendix — commercial formats, regulation, manufacturing, market

Research input for the Tarte "Iced Black at Home" kit plan. Compiled 13–14 Aug 2026.
Product: bundled kit — bag of Tarte house-blend grind (roasted by Parallel Roasters)
+ Tarte cold foam topping component (vanilla bean, dulce de leche, matcha).
Customer brews an iced long black / cold brew at home and tops it with the foam.

The dairy foam component carries essentially all of the manufacturing and
regulatory load; the coffee grind is shelf-stable and near-zero burden (§2.4).

## 1. Product formats & comparables

### 1A. Cold foam component — format options on the market

**(a) Aerosol / pressurized "shake-and-press" cold foam cans — the format that
defined the category (US, 2024–25).** N2O-propelled refrigerated cans with
proprietary dispensing tips (not liquid bottles):

Manufacturing: in-house big-dairy aerosol lines; no accessible Australian
aerosol-dairy co-pack path at café MOQs. **None of these are ranged in
Australia — genuine white space.**

**(b) Refrigerated liquid "froth-it-yourself" bottles — the lowest-capex format
and best fit for the Tarte kit.**
- Starbucks creamers (made under licence by Nestlé): 28 fl oz PET bottles,
  ~US$5–6, dairy + non-dairy (almond/oat), café-flavour positioning (Caramel
  Macchiato, Lavender Latte).
  https://www.nestleusa.com/media/pressreleases/allpressreleases/starbucks-non-dairy-creamers-launching-nationwide
  · https://www.target.com/p/starbucks-non-dairy-coffee-creamer-28-fl-oz/-/A-94664203
- Chobani Coffee Creamer: 24 fl oz bottles ~US$4–5, milk+cream base; "Chobani
  Sweet Cream into cold foam" is a TikTok discover topic — consumers already
  whip liquid creamers into foam with US$10–30 handheld frothers.
  https://www.chobani.com/products/creamers
  · https://www.tiktok.com/discover/chobani-sweet-cream-into-cold-foam
- Consumer aeration: handheld electric frother (US$10–30; TikTok Shop
  bestseller), French press pumping, mason-jar shake, benchtop frothers
  (Breville/Sunbeam widely owned in AU). A **branded Tarte mini-frother** is an
  obvious kit inclusion/upsell.

**(c) Nitro-widget self-foaming cans** — La Colombe Draft Latte (9 oz, ~US$3–4,
in-can N2O widget releases foam on opening;
https://www.lacolombe.com/pages/la-colombe-lattes), High Brew Nitro Sweet Cream
(https://www.highbrewcoffee.com/products/nitro-sweet-cream). Finished drinks,
specialised canning, can MOQs 2,000–5,000 cases/SKU — not viable at Tarte scale.

**(d) Shelf-stable UHT** — no global "cold foam" UHT SKU yet; nearest analogues
are barista milks engineered to foam (Oatly Barista cold-foam story:
https://www.greenqueen.com.hk/oatly-barista-cold-foam-non-dairy-plant-based-milk-coffee/).
6–12 mo ambient life, kills cold-chain cost for a national kit, but line
minimums ≥5,000 L (see §3).

**(e) Powdered/instant foam mixes** — Modernist Pantry "Foam Magic" (1–2% dose,
froths any liquid: https://modernistpantry.com/products/foam-magic.html);
shelf-stable whipping cream powders
(https://topcreamery.com/product/whipped-cream-powder-500g/). Cheapest to make
(dry-blend co-packers, tiny MOQs), ambient = trivially giftable/shippable, and
**matcha works especially well as powder**. Weakest "fresh café" story. A
powdered vanilla-bean / dulce / matcha foam sachet set inside the kit is the
fastest national-DTC route.

**(f) Café-filled grab-and-go foam + kit** — no large branded comparable found;
open territory. Cafés currently make cold foam via iSi whipper + N2O chargers
(method: https://www.creamright.com/blogs/whipping-things-up/how-to-make-cold-foam-at-home-using-a-whipped-cream-dispenser).
**Do not bundle N2O chargers in a consumer kit**: non-therapeutic N2O is
Schedule 6 nationally (Oct 2022), a "restricted substance" under QLD's
Medicines and Poisons Act 2019, with QLD consumer-retail sale restrictions and
18+ minimums
(https://monsterwhip.com/n2o-cream-charger-laws-in-australia-2026-are-nangs-legal-state-by-state-guide/).
Use electric frother instead.

## 2. Australian regulatory requirements

### 2.1 FSANZ labelling (Chapter 1, Part 1.2) — applies to each packaged component AND the bundle
- **Standard 1.2.1**: food made and packaged on the premises it's sold from is
  exempt from most labelling (allergen info must still be provided) — the
  in-café pilot advantage. Fully labelled packaging required once sold packaged
  beyond point of sale.
- **Standard 1.2.8 NIP** per serve + per 100 mL/100 g on the foam component and
  the coffee: https://foodlabelmaker.com/regulatory-hub/fsanz/nutrition-information-panel/
- **Standard 1.2.3 PEAL**: "Milk" bolded in ingredients + bold "Contains: Milk"
  statement (mandatory since 25 Feb 2024; transition ended Feb 2026):
  https://foodlabelmaker.com/regulatory-hub/fsanz/peal-allergen-labelling-guide/
- **Standard 1.2.5 date marking**: chilled foam → **Use By**; coffee grind and
  powdered foam → Best Before.
- Also: lot ID, name + street address, ingredient list, characterising
  ingredient % (e.g. % cream; % coffee for the grind bag), storage directions,
  Country of Origin standard mark. **Kit-specific note:** a multi-component
  retail bundle needs compliant labelling on each inner unit sold as part of
  the assembled pack, and the outer pack must not obscure mandatory
  information — practical guidance in QLD Health's "Label Buster":
  https://www.health.qld.gov.au/__data/assets/pdf_file/0016/441610/label-buster-guide-bus.pdf
  · FSANZ hub: https://www.foodstandards.gov.au/business/labelling
- **Standard 1.6.1** microbiological limits apply to the dairy foam component.
- Matcha flavour: plain matcha is fine; avoid added-caffeine formulations
  (formulated caffeinated beverage rules) — a matcha-dairy foam using tea
  powder is standard food, no extra burden.

### 2.2 Dairy foam component — Standard 4.2.4 + Safe Food Production Queensland (THE pivotal issue)
- **Standard 4.2.4** (Dairy PPP Standard) puts dairy *processors* under a
  documented HACCP-based food safety program, pasteurisation controls
  (72°C/15 s or equivalent), traceability/recall; administered in QLD by
  **SFPQ, not council**.
  https://www.foodstandards.gov.au/business/primary-production-and-processing
  · https://www.foodstandards.gov.au/publications/aguidetostandard424p5768
- **SFPQ Dairy Scheme**: accreditation required to "process dairy produce" for
  sale — listed processing includes pasteurising milk and producing **cream**,
  butter, cheese, yoghurt, ice-cream, powders; pasteurisation classed "higher
  risk". Annual calendar-year accreditation; application fee + annual fee;
  audits billed **A$352.20/hr + GST** in 15-min increments.
  https://www.safefood.qld.gov.au/food-business/accreditation/dairy-scheme/food-business-dairy-processor/
- **Key open question — flavoured cream foam base made from bought-in
  pasteurised milk/cream:**
  - SFPQ's "I don't see me" page excludes: manufacturing foods *containing*
    dairy as an ingredient (cakes, quiches, frozen meals) and **ice-cream made
    and sold retail from the same premises** (explicit retail exemption in the
    Food Production (Safety) Regulation 2014).
    https://www.safefood.qld.gov.au/food-business/accreditation/dairy-scheme/dairy-scheme-i-dont-see-me/
  - But a sweetened cream product arguably *is* dairy produce (cf. NSW, where
    flavoured milk manufacture is licensable dairy processing:
    https://www.foodauthority.nsw.gov.au/sites/default/files/_Documents/scienceandtechnical/dairy_food_safety_scheme_risk_assessment.pdf).
    Reasonable read: **in-café packed, same-premises retail = likely no
    accreditation (milkshake/gelato analogy); packaged wholesale/DTC bottles =
    likely accreditation required.** Get a written SFPQ determination
    (07 3253 9800) describing the exact process (cold-blending pasteurised
    inputs, no re-pasteurisation) and each channel. Regulation:
    https://www.legislation.qld.gov.au/view/whole/html/inforce/2022-05-08/sl-2014-0197
  - If SFPQ-accredited, that activity is **exempt from separate council
    licensing**:
    https://www.qld.gov.au/health/staying-healthy/food-pantry/starting-a-food-business/food-business-licences/do-i-need-a-food-business-licence
  - **Co-packing sidesteps this entirely**: if Norco (already accredited) makes
    the foam base, Tarte is a brand owner, not a dairy processor — the single
    biggest regulatory de-risking lever.

### 2.3 Council licensing & Standard 3.2.2A (City of Gold Coast — all three venues, one council)
- Existing café licences must be **amended to cover manufacturing/wholesale**;
  apply ~20 days ahead; wholesale manufacture requires a **recall system**.
  https://www.goldcoast.qld.gov.au/Services/Report-pay-apply/Permits-licensing/Food-business-%E2%80%93-fixed-mobile-temporary-premises
  · https://ablis.business.gov.au/service/qld/food-business-licence-gold-coast-city-council/5498
- **Standard 3.2.2A** Category One: certified Food Safety Supervisor (≤5 yrs
  old cert), food handler training, prescribed-activity records.
  https://www.goldcoast.qld.gov.au/Invest-do-business/Business-regulation/Food-safety-standard
  · https://blog.foodsafety.com.au/is-your-food-business-category-one-or-two-under-standard-3.2.2a

### 2.4 Coffee grind component — negligible burden
- QLD councils explicitly class **roasting/packaging coffee and grinding coffee
  beans as low-risk / non-potentially-hazardous**; sale of coffee beans or
  ground coffee is **exempt from food business licensing** (grinding itself
  exempt; note some councils flag that *packaging + wholesale distribution* may
  still need licensing — check with City of Gold Coast if Tarte repacks bulk
  grind rather than buying retail-ready bags).
  https://www.lgtoolbox.qld.gov.au/topics/food/do-i-need-licence
  · https://www.brisbane.qld.gov.au/laws-and-permits/food-business-licences/running-a-food-business
- Cleanest structure: **Parallel Roasters supplies finished, labelled "Roasted
  by Parallel for Tarte" bags** — Parallel keeps manufacturing compliance;
  Tarte's obligations reduce to correct supplier details on label and normal
  Food Act duties. Coffee needs Best Before + CoOL but no NIP-critical
  allergens and no cold chain.
- Result: kit logistics are governed entirely by the foam component — chilled
  kit if foam is fresh (courier ≤5°C, Standard 3.2.2), ambient national kit if
  foam is powdered/UHT.

### 2.5 Channel rules recap
- **Own-store retail** (components made/packed on premises): council amendment
  only; partial labelling exemption.
- **Wholesale**: full labelling, recall plan, likely SFPQ accreditation for the
  foam (unless co-packed), buyer demands (HACCP cert, A$10–20 M product
  liability).
- **Online/DTC chilled**: Standard 3.2.2 ≤5°C through delivery + 2hr/4hr rule;
  demonstrate control (gel packs/liners/data loggers). Australian Cold Chain
  Guidelines 2017:
  https://fpsc-anz.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/03/Australian-Cold-Chain-Guidelines-2017.pdf
  · https://afgc.org.au/nutrition-and-regulation/regulation-and-resources/cold-chain-guidelines/
  · packaging: https://envirofreeze.com.au/cold-chain-knowledge/cold-chain-packaging-food-businesses-australia/
  · CoR liability: https://aussietridentgroup.com.au/cold-chain-logistics-in-australia/
- A 7–14-day use-by chilled foam realistically caps DTC at SEQ/northern NSW
  next-day zones; powder/UHT foam unlocks national gifting.

### Bundle pricing logic
Anchor maths for the kit:
- Components at plausible standalone RRP: 250 g Tarte grind A$18–22 (specialty
  retail norm; ST. ALi 500 g = A$31–38 at Coles); cold foam topper (500 mL
  chilled bottle or 3-sachet powder set) A$12–16; branded mini frother A$15–20.
- **Kit precedent bands:** bubble tea kits A$20–35 (multi-serve, per-serve
  savings pitch); coffee+hardware kits A$60–100 (Industry Beans, CMMNT);
  premium café-branded gift kits A$85–159 (Messina x Cocktail Porter, 6–18
  serves).
- Suggested architecture: **core kit A$39–49** (grind + one foam flavour +
  recipe card; ~8–10 serves → ~A$4–5/serve vs A$7–8 café equivalent = the
  headline claim), **deluxe/gift kit A$59–75** (grind + all three foam flavours
  + frother + glass/cup), foam-only refills A$12–15 and grind refills A$18–22
  to build repeat purchase. Kits should price at a modest (~10–15%) discount to
  summed components — enough to reward the bundle, not enough to cannibalise
  refill margin. Specialty food GM benchmark 40–60% (premium beverages 50–60%):
  https://blog.gourmetfoodmarketplace.com/gross-profit-margins-by-category-a-guide-to-maximizing-retail-profitability
- Own-venue/DTC first: capture full retail margin. If wholesaling later:
  retailer takes 30–45% of shelf, distributor 20–30%, trade spend 15–25% of
  gross — need ≥35–50% GM at sell-in or distributed retail loses money per
  case: https://cpgcanary.ai/guides/retail-margins/ · AU supermarket avg
  product margin ~26–27%:
  https://www.agriculture.gov.au/sites/default/files/sitecollectiondocuments/ag-food/publications/price-determin/section5.pdf

### Gifting & retail-counter merchandising
- Boba Luxe built its whole model on kits-as-**gifts**; Messina's A$85–159 kits
