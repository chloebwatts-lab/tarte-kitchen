# Tarte Kitchen audit, September 2026

Question asked: are we using Tarte Kitchen well, and is it turning
information into more profit, lower wages, lower COGS and less work?

## Bottom line

Tarte Kitchen is a strong measurement system with a small number of loops
that genuinely change a decision, and a long tail of screens that report
a number and stop. The finance numbers for Currumbin moved the right way
over winter. The tool cannot claim all of that, but the two ratios it
targets are the two that improved. The biggest gaps are not missing
features. They are a real-time labour page that nobody can reach, a
menu-price step that was never built, and a pricing pipeline that has
needed a forensic script every few weeks.

## Strengths: where a number changes a decision

1. Price alerts to recipe cost. Accepting an alert rewrites the ingredient
   price, logs history and recosts every recipe. The most carefully
   engineered subsystem, and the one with the most bug history.
2. Department ordering. Each section orders its own during the day, the
   end-of-day sheet regroups by supplier and sends one email per
   supplier. Direct labour and error saving.
3. Live spend and the Sunday email. Real invoices against forecast times
   target COGS, per venue, landing before Monday ordering.
4. Par levels and suggested orders. Chef-accepted pars from usage and
   delivery cadence, stocktake-aware suggestions with an honest reason
   string when there is no stocktake.
5. Reviews. Drafted by Claude, approved by Shawna, posted, with a 28-day
   response watch.
6. Wastage analytics and the shrinkage detective. Reported waste against
   stocktake variance gives a dollar figure per ingredient for the
   portioning or theft conversation.
7. The weekly digest architecture. All arithmetic in code, Claude writes
   only prose and action items from a typed snapshot. Copy this pattern.
8. Discipline. One TODO in the whole source tree, nearly no dead
   components, every dangerous path carries a comment explaining the
   incident that produced it.

## Weaknesses, ranked by cost

1. `/labour/live` is orphaned. It computes in-week spend, projected
   end-of-week wage % and variance against the department bands. It is
   the only lever that lets a manager cut a Saturday shift instead of
   reading about it on Friday. Nothing links to it. One line in the
   sidebar.
2. The COGS chain stops one step short. Invoice, price alert, recipe cost,
   food-cost %, menu matrix: every link works, and no screen ever says
   "raise this from $24.00 to $25.50 to get back to 28%". Selling prices
   are maintained by a hardcoded script. Every food-cost and GP figure sits
   on top of that.
3. Pricing pipeline fragility. Two alert systems, five places a pack size
   lives, reciprocal conversion factors, per-line regex parsing, and a
   product code that was extracted and thrown away. Detailed in
   `docs/pricing-rebuild.md`, with the rebuild on this branch.
4. Investigation is the operating cost. 182 scripts, 95 of them one-off
   probes and fixes, seven for a single misattribution day. Each is an
   hour where a number on screen could not be trusted. Several of them
   (own invoices vs the bookkeeper's COGS, forecast vs actual, revenue
   gap) are reconciliation reports the app should run nightly and does
   not.
5. Two schedules. `vercel.json` lists crons that are not what production
   runs. The Google Sheets wastage sync exists only there, so it never
   fires. `/reports` reads a table only an unscheduled sync writes.
6. Tea Garden is a second-class venue: no labour targets, COGS folded into
   Beach House, wastage sheet defaulted to Beach House, a hardcoded 50%
   pastry revenue share.
7. Nothing measures the tool's own payback. Weekly impact is computed per
   price alert and never summed into "accepted alerts saved $X this
   quarter". Forecast accuracy is never scored. Stocktake cadence is not
   enforced, so the two best analyses quietly degrade.
8. Data-quality traps that make a money number wrong without anyone
   noticing: historical purchase-price corruption from two documented
   incidents, ingredient prices that go stale when alerts are dismissed,
   invoice venue attribution that has failed before, seeded example
   food-safety runs mixed with real ones.

## Recommended next moves, in order

1. Link `/labour/live` in the sidebar. Half an hour.
2. Add a food-cost target per menu category and a recommended selling
   price on dishes and the menu matrix, with accept writing
   `Dish.sellingPrice`. Retires the selling-price script.
3. Run the pricing rebuild in shadow for two weeks, answer the pack
   questions, compare against v2, then cut over. Steps in
   `docs/pricing-rebuild.md`.
4. One nightly reconciliation cron with four checks: own-invoice COGS vs
   bookkeeper COGS, POS implied price vs `Dish.sellingPrice`, ingredient
   price age, days since last stocktake. One digest section.
5. Delete `vercel.json` or make it the schedule. Decide whether the
   Google Sheets wastage tabs are still used; if so schedule the sync, if
   not delete it. Delete the two endpoints that call themselves temporary.
6. Give Tea Garden its own labour bands and its own wastage tab.
7. Sum accepted-alert impact per quarter and show it on the price alerts
   page, so the tool reports its own return.
