# The Tarte Training System

*For Chloe and managers. This is the operating manual for how we train, keep and sharpen the team. Staff never see this document, they see the booklets.*

Built on the three best-documented service cultures in the world, translated to Tarte:

- **Ritz-Carlton:** a credo everyone carries, a daily line-up that re-teaches the culture forever, and staff empowered to fix problems on the spot.
- **Chick-fil-A:** four checkable behaviours, scripted language, a certified-trainer layer that owns every new hire's first shifts.
- **Danny Meyer:** hire 51% warmth 49% skill, and treat every mistake as a chance to write a great final chapter.

## Why this shape

People forget 70% of what they read within a day. So the system is not "read a manual once". It is:

1. **A short booklet on day one** (the department booklet + The Tarte Way).
2. **A named trainer for the first 3 shifts** who signs off skills one by one.
3. **A 5-minute line-up every day** that rotates one value at a time, forever.
4. **A one-page Reset Card** when someone goes flat, instead of a lecture.

The line-up carries the culture. The booklet just starts it.

## The pieces

| Piece | What | When |
|---|---|---|
| The Tarte Way | 4-page all-staff booklet: motto, Core 4, phrasebook, HEARD recovery, brand facts | Given at hire, before first shift |
| Department booklet | Their job, sequence of service, upsells, non-negotiables, sign-off grid | Given at hire |
| Sign-off grid | Skills ticked off by their trainer across first 2 weeks | Back page of every booklet |
| Reset Card | The last page of every booklet. One page. Reissued when standards slip | As needed + every 6 months |
| Daily line-up | 5 minutes at open. Numbers, 86s, push item, one Tarte Ten value | Every shift, every venue |

## Hiring filter (before training starts)

Danny Meyer's 51% rule: hire warmth, optimism, curiosity, work ethic, self-awareness. Skills can be taught in two weeks, personality cannot. In trials, watch for one thing above all: **do they notice people?** A trial who spots the empty water glass unprompted beats one who makes a perfect coffee.

## First two weeks (the pathway)

- **Before shift 1:** booklet sent, read The Tarte Way + their department booklet. 20 minutes total, that is deliberate.
- **Shifts 1 to 3:** paired with a named trainer, never "just watch". Every skill is taught Tell, Show, Do, Review: explain it, demonstrate it, they do it while talking it through, correct on the spot. If the worker has not learned, the trainer has not taught.
- **End of week 1:** trainer asks the 5 quiz questions in the booklet, verbally, 3 minutes.
- **End of week 2:** sign-off grid complete, manager countersigns. They are now certified for that department.
- **Day 30:** manager check-in. Two questions: what is confusing, what would you change. Log training completion in Tarte Kitchen (`/kitchen/training`) alongside the food-safety record.

**Trainers:** pick 1 to 2 per venue per department. It is a badge, not a chore: name them in the roster, mention it in reviews, pay a trainer loading if possible. Teaching a standard is the strongest way to re-commit someone to it.

## The daily line-up (the engine)

5 minutes, standing, before open, same spot daily. Agenda never changes:

1. **Numbers** (30 sec): yesterday's take or review score, one sentence.
2. **Today** (2 min): bookings, functions, 86s, weather call, who is where.
3. **Push item** (30 sec): the one thing we name to guests today, and why. Taste it when it is a new bake.
4. **One value** (90 sec): the next Tarte Ten value in rotation. Read it, one real example from this week, done. Ten values means the whole culture is re-taught every two weeks, forever.
5. **Shout-out** (30 sec): any staff name from this week's Google reviews gets read out loud. Tarte Kitchen already extracts these automatically.

Fridays: read the best and worst review of the week in full. No names on the worst.

## The Tarte Ten

Written as commitments, not policies. These rotate through line-ups and are printed in every booklet.

1. I greet every guest within 30 seconds, even if it is just eye contact and "won't be a moment".
2. I know what is in the cabinet today, and I have tasted it.
3. I name one thing. I never ask "anything else?"
4. I tell guests the wait before they ask.
5. I ask about allergies early, and treat the answer as law.
6. I own problems: hear, empathise, apologise, resolve. I never argue.
7. My section is spotless without being asked.
8. Meals land together, hot, complete. If they cannot, I say so first.
9. I help the team, but my section comes first.
10. I farewell every guest by choice, not by accident, and invite them back.

## Recovery and empowerment

Every booklet carries **HEARD**: Hear them out, Empathise, Apologise (sincerely, regardless of fault), Resolve on the spot, Delight with a small extra touch if you can. The standing Delight gift is a **cruller**: a cinnamon cruller costs about $0.52 in ingredients against $7 of perceived value, is low labour (unlike the scroll and kouign amann), and is the signature. Cheapest wow in the building.

**Standing empowerment:** any staff member may remake or replace any item on the spot, no manager needed, no interrogation of the guest. Comping money off a bill stays a manager call. *Recommendation for Chloe: consider a formal per-guest limit (Ritz-Carlton pre-authorises $2,000 per guest per day; a Tarte figure of $50 would end most bad reviews at the table). This coexists with the review-reply rule: we never offer freebies in writing after the fact, we fix things in the room.*

Why this matters here specifically: in our own review data the single most repeated phrase in 1-star reviews is not about food. It is "no apology". Dismissiveness, not mistakes, writes our worst reviews.

## Keeping tenured staff sharp (the complacency system)

Complacency is a system gap, not a character flaw. Triggers for action:

| Trigger | Action |
|---|---|
| Named in a negative review, complaint pattern, mystery-shop fail | Reset Card conversation within 48 hours |
| New menu cycle | 10-minute line-up extension, taste everything new |
| 6-month anniversary | Reset Card reissued as routine, framed as a refresher not a warning |
| Standards drifting venue-wide | Manager runs one full week of line-ups personally |

The Reset Card conversation is 5 minutes, private, and starts with recognition of what they do well. It is re-onboarding, not discipline. If two Reset conversations do not land, that is a performance conversation instead.

**Mystery shopper (recommended):** one visit per venue per fortnight, scored against the Core 4 + that department's non-negotiables. Tiered team reward so one person's slip cannot zero everyone. Named individual praise passes straight to line-up.

## The scoreboard

The system is measurable because Tarte Kitchen already tags every Google review by theme and staff name:

- **Weekly:** review count, average, SERVICE and WAIT_TIME negative-theme counts per venue. These two themes are the training system's KPIs. Current baseline (all-time): Beach House 4.55 across 1,688 reviews, Burleigh 4.39 across 1,394. Service is the #1 negative theme at both.
- **Staff mentions:** positive name-mentions per month is the recognition metric. Post the count, read the names.
- **Target:** every department's booklet exists to move exactly these numbers.

## Week 1 quiz answer key (managers only)

Each booklet ends with 5 verbal quiz questions. Ask them casually at the end of week 1, 3 minutes. The answers:

**FOH Burleigh.** Crullers are twisted choux, fried and glazed, our signature. Sourdough (and croissants) come from a local artisan baker. Instead of "anything else?" you name one specific thing. H and E: Hear them out, Empathise. Today's kitchen wait: they must know the live number, that is the point of the question.

**FOH Beach House.** Four spaces: the Cafe on the sand, the Restaurant, the Tea Garden, The Hideout. Cafe opens 6:30am (Restaurant 7am). Dinner: "not yet, but watch this space." Water lands before drinks. Crab: Queensland spanner crab, hand picked. Staggered mains: the table hears it from us before the first plate lands.

**Tea Garden.** Sitting is 90 minutes. $2.50 is the cloud drink upgrade. At seating you confirm booked dietaries (and the occasion). Closed Mon and Tue. A baby shower table gets the private high tea pitch (Tea Garden or The Hideout).

**Barista.** Parallel Roasters, exclusive house blend. Ceremonial grade matcha. Off-tasting latte: remake it, cheerfully, no debate. Cold foams: vanilla bean, dulce de leche, matcha. 7am flat white pairs with a muffin top (any confident pastry answer passes).

**Kitchen.** Staggered table: FOH is told before the food moves. Allergen matrix: in Tarte Kitchen, printed at the pass. Cold under 5°C, hot over 60°C. 86 early, when the item is at risk, not when it is gone. FSS names are on the noticeboard, they must know them.

**Pastry.** Imperfect product: you mess it up, you do not use it, wastage log with the real reason, never the shelf. Muffin tops and cookies outsell everything because they are perfect every time. Allergen changes: flagged to the matrix the same day. At open you tell FOH what is singing. Taste answer: any honest answer about tasting the restock passes, "I have not" fails.
