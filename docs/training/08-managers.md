# Manager Booklet: Shift Leaders & Managers

*Anyone who runs a shift. Read everything else first, this is how you keep it alive. 8 minutes.*

## Your job in one line

Staff treat guests the way managers treat staff. Ritz-Carlton calls their people "Ladies and Gentlemen serving Ladies and Gentlemen" because dignity flows downhill. Every standard in these booklets survives only if the person running the shift models it, coaches it and celebrates it, daily.

## The daily line-up (never skip it)

5 minutes, standing, before open, same spot. This is the highest-leverage 5 minutes of your day, it is how the culture gets re-taught forever:

1. **Numbers** (30 sec): one sentence from yesterday.
2. **Today** (2 min): bookings, functions, 86s, weather, sections.
3. **Push item** (30 sec): the one thing everyone names today. New bake? Everyone tastes it.
4. **One Tarte Ten value** (90 sec): read it, one real story from this week, move on. Ten values, two-week cycle, forever.
5. **Shout-out** (30 sec): staff named in this week's Google reviews get read out. Tarte Kitchen pulls these automatically.

Friday: read the week's best review in full, and the worst without names.

## Coaching: Tell, Show, Do, Review

Never "just watch Sarah for a shift". Every skill, four beats: explain it and why, demonstrate it, they do it while talking through it, correct immediately and kindly. If they have not learned it, you have not taught it yet. You own the sign-off grids: no grid complete, no solo section.

Coach against steps, not vibes. "You skipped the check-back" lands. "Lift your energy" does not.

## Recovery: you are the backstop

Staff run HEARD and can remake anything on the spot. You get called for money off, table disputes, allergy incidents, anything past two minutes. Your standards:

- **Land within 60 seconds of being called.** The wait for a manager is part of the complaint.
- **Apologise first, even mid-facts.** Our worst reviews all contain "no apology". Never re-litigate the guest's story.
- **Reserved-table failures, big-group refusals, rain re-seats:** you personally own the outcome, find the alternative, and check back on that table twice.
- **Write the final chapter.** A guest whose problem was fixed brilliantly reviews better than one who had no problem.
- Complaints that leave the venue unresolved go to hello@tarte.com.au follow-up via Chloe, same day.

## Reset conversations (the complacency system)

When someone goes flat, the tool is the Reset Card, not a lecture:

- **Triggers:** named in a negative review, a complaint pattern, mystery-shop miss, or simply six months served.
- **The conversation:** 5 minutes, private, same day-ish. Start with what they are genuinely good at. Hand them the card: "this is the reset, it is the same card everyone gets, run it fresh tomorrow." Re-onboarding, not discipline.
- Two resets that do not land become a performance conversation. Do not let the card become a threat, it dies the day it does.

## Your scoreboards

- **Reviews:** Tarte Kitchen tags every review by theme and staff name. Your two KPIs are the SERVICE and WAIT_TIME negative counts. Baseline: Beach House 4.55 (1,688 reviews), Burleigh 4.39 (1,394). Service is the #1 complaint at both, which means it is the #1 opportunity, it is free to fix.
- **Wages:** roster and live wage % against the venue's department targets in Tarte Shifts. Publish rosters on time, staff plan lives around them.
- **Compliance:** checklist runs, temps and cooling logs complete daily in Tarte Kitchen. Chase blanks the same day, they are legal records.
- **Wastage:** logged with honest reasons. Patterns go to pars and prep sheets, not to blame.

## Recognition (do it loudly)

- Review name-mentions read at line-up, always.
- Catch someone doing it right once per shift, say it specifically and publicly. "Great save on table 12" beats "good job today".
- Trainers are a status, name them, thank them, push their names forward for anything extra going.

## Non-negotiables for the person in charge

1. Line-up runs every single open. No exceptions, including yours.
2. No solo sections before the sign-off grid is complete.
3. You land within 60 seconds of a recovery call.
4. Rosters published on time, breaks protected.
5. Compliance blanks chased same day.
6. Praise in public, resets in private.
7. You model the Core 4 on your worst day. Especially on your worst day.

## RESET CARD: Managers *(yes, you get one too)*

**Dignity flows downhill.**

- Run the line-up. 5 minutes. Today.
- Coach the step, not the vibe.
- 60 seconds to a complaint. Apologise first.
- Read the review names out loud.
- Reset cards, not lectures. Praise, specific and public.
- The shift copies your energy. Choose it at the door.
